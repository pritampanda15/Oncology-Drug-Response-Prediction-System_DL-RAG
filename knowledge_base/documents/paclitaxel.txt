Paclitaxel Mechanism and Response Biomarkers
Paclitaxel is a taxane that stabilizes microtubules, preventing their depolymerization during cell division. This disrupts mitotic spindle function, causing cell cycle arrest at G2/M phase and subsequent apoptosis. Paclitaxel binds to beta-tubulin subunits within assembled microtubules.

Key genes affecting paclitaxel response:

TUBB3: Beta-tubulin class III is associated with paclitaxel resistance. High TUBB3 expression alters microtubule dynamics and reduces drug binding affinity. TUBB3 overexpression is observed in resistant ovarian and lung cancers.

ABCB1 (MDR1): ATP-binding cassette transporter encoding P-glycoprotein, a major efflux pump for taxanes. ABCB1 overexpression actively pumps paclitaxel out of cells, reducing intracellular concentration and causing multidrug resistance.

ABCC1 (MRP1): Another efflux transporter contributing to paclitaxel resistance through drug export.

CYP2C8: Cytochrome P450 enzyme responsible for paclitaxel metabolism. CYP2C8 polymorphisms affect drug clearance and toxicity. Poor metabolizers experience increased toxicity.

CYP3A4: Secondary metabolizing enzyme for paclitaxel. Drug interactions affecting CYP3A4 alter paclitaxel pharmacokinetics.

BCL2: Anti-apoptotic protein. High BCL2 expression can prevent paclitaxel-induced apoptosis, conferring resistance.

TP53: Functional p53 is required for optimal apoptotic response to paclitaxel-induced mitotic arrest. TP53 mutations may reduce sensitivity.

MAP4: Microtubule-associated protein 4 regulates microtubule stability. Altered MAP4 phosphorylation affects paclitaxel sensitivity.

BRCA1: BRCA1 deficiency may increase paclitaxel sensitivity in some contexts by impairing DNA damage repair during mitotic stress.

Clinical considerations: Paclitaxel is used in breast, ovarian, lung, and head and neck cancers. Peripheral neuropathy and myelosuppression are major toxicities. Albumin-bound paclitaxel (nab-paclitaxel) has improved delivery to tumors. Docetaxel is a related taxane with similar mechanisms but different pharmacokinetics.
