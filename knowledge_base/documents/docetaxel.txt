Docetaxel and Microtubule Dynamics

Docetaxel is a taxane chemotherapy agent that stabilizes microtubules, preventing their depolymerization. This blocks mitotic spindle function, causing cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase and subsequent apoptosis.

Key genes affecting docetaxel response:

TUBB3: Beta-tubulin class III is the primary drug target. High TUBB3 expression is consistently associated with taxane resistance across multiple cancer types. TUBB3 overexpression alters microtubule dynamics, reducing docetaxel binding affinity.

ABCB1 (MDR1/P-glycoprotein): This efflux transporter actively pumps taxanes out of cells. ABCB1 overexpression is a major mechanism of acquired taxane resistance. Tumors with high baseline ABCB1 may show primary resistance.

ABCC1 (MRP1): Another efflux transporter contributing to multidrug resistance. Works synergistically with ABCB1 to reduce intracellular drug accumulation.

MAP4: Microtubule-associated protein 4 regulates microtubule stability. Phosphorylation status of MAP4 affects taxane sensitivity.

STMN1 (Stathmin): A microtubule-destabilizing protein. High stathmin expression opposes taxane action and correlates with resistance.

BCL2: Anti-apoptotic protein that can prevent taxane-induced cell death. BCL2 overexpression is associated with reduced taxane efficacy.

CYP3A4: Primary enzyme metabolizing docetaxel. Genetic polymorphisms and drug interactions affecting CYP3A4 activity alter docetaxel pharmacokinetics and toxicity.

Clinical considerations: Docetaxel is used in breast, prostate, gastric, head and neck, and non-small cell lung cancers. Neutropenia is the primary dose-limiting toxicity. Fluid retention and peripheral neuropathy are also common adverse effects.
